Build a small library package named "base64": encode and strictly decode RFC 4648 base64 (section 4, the standard alphabet, with padding). It is a pure library: no network, no database, no files, no environment. Use only the standard library; do not depend on any other package. Every function is pure and every function has examples.

The package exposes exactly two functions:

- base64_encode(data) : the base64 text of data, as ASCII bytes. Never fails. Inputs: data is bytes. Result: bytes.
- base64_decode(text) : the original bytes, or Err(message) if text is not exactly the base64 that base64_encode would produce for some data. Inputs: text is bytes. Result: bytes, or an error message.

Alphabet. The 64 characters are A-Z (values 0-25), a-z (26-51), 0-9 (52-61), "+" (62) and "/" (63). The URL-safe characters "-" and "_" are NOT part of it. Padding is "=".

Encoding. Take the input three bytes at a time; every full group of 3 bytes becomes 4 characters. A final group of 2 bytes becomes 3 characters followed by one "=", and a final group of 1 byte becomes 2 characters followed by "==". The empty input encodes to the empty text. There are no line breaks, ever, and the output is always padded.

Decoding is strict. The empty text decodes to the empty data. Anything else must be a whole number of 4-character groups where only the last group may end in "=" or "==", and every one of the following is an Err (and decoding must never panic, on any bytes):
- wrong length: a text whose length is not a multiple of 4. This includes text with its padding left off: "Zg" and "Zm8" are Err.
- a character outside the alphabet: any other byte at all, including "-" and "_", space, tab, "\r" and "\n" (whitespace is NOT skipped, before, inside or after the text), NUL, and bytes of 128 or more.
- misplaced or missing padding: "=" anywhere except as the last one or two characters of the text ("Zg==Zg==" and "=AAA" and "A=AA" are Err), three or four "=" in a group ("A===" and "===="), and a single "=" in the third place of the last group ("AA=A"). A last group with two data characters must be followed by exactly "==", with three data characters by exactly one "=".
- non-zero trailing bits: the bits that padding throws away must be zero, so the text must be exactly what base64_encode gives. In a last group "xy==" the 4 low bits of the value of y must be 0 (so y is one of A, Q, g, w) and in a last group "xyz=" the 2 low bits of the value of z must be 0 (so z is one of A E I M Q U Y c g k o s w 0 4 8). "Zh==" and "Zm9=" are Err, "Zg==" and "Zm8=" are Ok.

Worked examples (the answers are exact), the vectors of RFC 4648 section 10:

- base64_encode("") is ""; "f" is "Zg=="; "fo" is "Zm8="; "foo" is "Zm9v"; "foob" is "Zm9vYg=="; "fooba" is "Zm9vYmE="; "foobar" is "Zm9vYmFy".
- base64_decode of each of those texts is Ok of the original: "" gives Ok(""), "Zg==" gives Ok("f"), "Zm9vYmFy" gives Ok("foobar").
- base64_decode("Zg"), base64_decode("Zm9vYg="), base64_decode("Zg==\n"), base64_decode("Zm9v YmFy"), base64_decode("Zm9-"), base64_decode("Zg==Zg=="), base64_decode("Zh==") and base64_decode("Zm9=") are all Err.

Properties that must hold and be checked: for every data, base64_decode(base64_encode(data)) is Ok(data); base64_encode(data) has length 4 * ceil(len(data) / 3); base64_decode never panics on any bytes.
